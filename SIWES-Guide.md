SIWES Report Standard Outline

I. Front Matter (Preliminary Pages)
    • Title Page: Includes the University name, Department, Title of the Report (e.g., "A Technical Report on the Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme Undertaken at [Company Name]"), Your Full Name, Matriculation Number, and Date of Submission.[2][3][4][5][6]
    • Declaration: A formal statement by you confirming that the work is original and was carried out by you during the attachment.
    • Certification: A page for signatures.[1][5][6] It usually requires the signature of your Industry-Based Supervisor, your Departmental SIWES Coordinator, and sometimes the Head of Department (HOD) to certify that the report is valid.[1]
    • Dedication: A brief note dedicating the report to someone special (e.g., parents or mentors).
    • Acknowledgement: Thanking the people and organizations that helped you (ITF, the company, your supervisors, lecturers, and family).
    • Abstract: A concise summary (usually one page) of the entire report.[1] It should mention where you worked, the key technologies/tools you used, the main tasks you performed, and the overall outcome of the training.
    • Table of Contents: A list of all chapters and sub-sections with their corresponding page numbers.
    • List of Tables/Figures: If you have used diagrams, screenshots, or tables, list them here.
II. Main Body (Chapters)
Chapter One: Introduction
    • 1.1 Background of SIWES: A brief history and purpose of the SIWES program (mentioning the Industrial Training Fund - ITF).
    • 1.2 Aims and Objectives of SIWES: Why the program exists (e.g., bridging the gap between theory and practice).
    • 1.3 Importance of the Training: How it contributes to your professional development as a Computer Scientist.
Chapter Two: Organization Profile
    • 2.1 Brief History of the Company: When it was founded and its growth.
    • 2.2 Mission and Vision Statement: The core goals of the organization.
    • 2.3 Organizational Structure (Organogram): A chart showing the hierarchy of the company (from CEO down to interns).[1]
    • 2.4 Departments and Functions: A description of the different units within the company and how they operate.
    • 2.5 Services/Products Offered: What the company actually does (e.g., software development, networking, IT support, etc.).[1][6]
Chapter Three: Work Experience (The Core Technical Chapter)
    • This is the most important part for a Computer Science student. Do not just say "I worked on computers." Be specific.
    • 3.1 Description of Tasks Assigned: A chronological or thematic account of your roles.
    • 3.2 Tools, Technologies, and Frameworks Used: List the software, hardware, and languages you worked with (e.g., Python, React, MySQL, Cisco Routers, VS Code, Windows Server, etc.).
    • 3.3 Technical Details/Projects Handled:
        ◦ If you did web development, explain the architecture or show snippets of the logic.
        ◦ If you did networking, describe the topology or the configuration process.
        ◦ If you did hardware/maintenance, describe the troubleshooting steps.
        ◦ Tip: Use diagrams, flowcharts, or screenshots to illustrate your work.[1]
Chapter Four: Skills Gained and Challenges
    • 4.1 Technical Skills Acquired: New programming languages, debugging skills, database management, etc.[1]
    • 4.2 Soft Skills Acquired: Teamwork, communication, time management, and professional ethics.[1]
    • 4.3 Challenges Encountered: Difficulties you faced (e.g., complex coding errors, lack of specific hardware, or difficult project requirements).
    • 4.4 Solutions Provided: How you overcame those challenges (e.g., using documentation, seeking mentorship, or online research).
Chapter Five: Summary, Conclusion, and Recommendations
    • 5.1 Summary: A brief recap of your overall experience.
    • 5.2 Conclusion: A final thought on how the internship has prepared you for your career.
    • 5.3 Recommendations:
        ◦ To the Company (e.g., "Provide more modern hardware for interns").
        ◦ To the University/SIWES Unit (e.g., "Incorporate more practical coding into the curriculum").
        ◦ To Future Students (e.g., "Learn basic Python before starting the attachment").
III. Back Matter
    • References: If you cited any books, websites, or manuals in your report.
    • Appendices: Include supplementary material like:
        ◦ Copies of your weekly logbook entries.[1]
        ◦ Code snippets (if allowed).
        ◦ System diagrams or network topologies.[1]
        ◦ Photos of you at the workplace (if appropriate).[1]
Pro-Tips for Computer Science Students:
    1. Be Technical: Use professional terminology. Instead of saying "I fixed a website," say "I debugged front-end issues using Chrome DevTools and optimized CSS rendering."
    2. Use Visuals: A report with only text is boring. Include screenshots of code, database schemas, or network diagrams.
    3. Formatting Matters: Use Times New Roman, 12pt font size, and 1.5 or 2.0 line spacing. Ensure your headings are clear and consistent.
    4. Consult your Coordinator: Before printing the final copy, always show your draft to your Departmental SIWES Coordinator to ensure they don't have a specific template preference.
